# Coplanar: Architecture and Construction on the Same Plane

Visual identity and graphic system for an architecture and construction practice, built around alignment, precision and the relationship between planes.

**By Philipe Bastos · Visual Identity / Graphic Design**

![Coplanar identity applied to business cards](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PhilBastos/COPLANAR/main/assets/coplanar-guide/book-39.webp)

## The project

Coplanar starts from the idea that design and construction should operate together. The identity translates this approach into alignment, typography and relationships between planes, balancing technical rigor with understated warmth.

The starting point is coplanarity: distinct elements sharing the same plane. A horizontal and a diagonal make this concept recognizable across different formats.

## The challenge

The design challenge was to connect the practice's architectural thinking with its everyday communication. The system needed to remain coherent across project presentations, technical documents, stationery and digital content, while giving each format enough flexibility to serve its purpose.

## A precise signature

The horizontal wordmark is the primary signature. Its customized P adds a distinctive detail and provides a starting point for the graphic language. A stacked version supports formats where a horizontal signature is less suitable.

The identity guidelines define clear space using the P as a reference and specify minimum widths of 20 mm in print and 175 px in digital applications. These rules help preserve the signature's proportions and legibility.

## One family, different scales

Bricolage Grotesque carries headlines, highlights and body text. Hierarchy comes from changes in weight, scale and proportion, bringing continuity to both expressive compositions and practical documents.

The guidelines support proportional scales based on a 1.618 ratio. Segoe UI is the specified alternative for email, shared documents and environments where the primary family is unavailable.

## A graphic system derived from the wordmark

The relationship between horizontal and diagonal grows out of the logo and recurs throughout the compositions. Alignment provides continuity as the format changes.

Micrographics, including codes, markers and technical elements, help organize information without competing with the signature. Together, these resources connect the visual identity to the language of architectural practice.
